Few angling experiences rival gliding across a shallow saltwater grass flat in ten inches of crystal-clear water, watching tailing redfish root through the mud.

Kayaks provide the ultimate stealth vehicle for inshore saltwater angling. With no hull slap or engine rumble, you can stalk within effortless casting distance of pressured fish.

## 1. Timing the Tides

In inshore marshes, water movement dictates fish feeding behavior far more than time of day:

- **Incoming Tide**: Redfish push deep onto flooded spartina grass flats and oyster mounds to forage for fiddler crabs and juvenile shrimp.
- **Outgoing Tide**: Fish retreat into deep marsh drains and creek mouths to ambush bait flushed from the shallows. Anchor at the mouth of drains and cast uptide.

## 2. Stealth Approaches: Staking Out Quietly

When you spot cruising schools or nervous water, eliminate all paddle movement. Keep a fiberglass stakeout pole in hand to slide into the bottom silently. Dropping an anchor with a metal chain will instantly clear the flat.

## 3. Top Inshore Lure Presentations

- **Weedless Paddle Tails**: 3-to-4 inch paddletails rigged on a 1/8 oz weedless twist-lock hook allow you to bounce over oyster shells without snagging.
- **Topwater Walk-the-Dog**: Early morning mullet imitations provoke violent reaction strikes from aggressive speckled trout along channel edges.
- **Gold Spoons**: The timeless classic for stained water or choppy conditions, throwing maximum flash and vibration.
